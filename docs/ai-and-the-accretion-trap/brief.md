## What the essay argues

When a new computing primitive arrives broken (packets that get dropped, a protocol that forgets you between clicks, a web with no editor, a language written in ten days, a model that guesses the next word), the people best placed to judge it are the experts in the thing it replaces, and they judge the naked primitive against their mature system. They are usually right about the facts and wrong about the future, because what decides a primitive's fate is not how broken it is but what it makes cheap, and whether the scaffolding that fixes the brokenness can be built around it for less than the old system's rigidity costs. The AI argument of 2023–2026 has the same shape. The honest version of the thesis must also say when the skeptics were right (most hyped primitives die), and give the reader a test rather than a side.

## Angle and shape
